# Mutual fund domain primer (India)

Claude knows how Indian mutual funds are structured and how MFDs talk to clients. Use that knowledge to **frame, order and explain**. Do not use it to **state**. Anything that is a number, a limit, a date, a rule in force, a ranking or "what is happening now" must come from an approved fact in the ledger. Rules and market data change; this file does not.

## How to use it

- Step 1 (research): decide which official source can answer the topic, and which terms to search for.
- Step 2 (ideation): check that an angle uses the right concept and the right audience level.
- Step 4 (script): choose the plain word for a technical term, and avoid mixing concepts (for example NAV movement vs SIP contribution vs AUM).

## Stable concepts (definitions only)

- **Mutual fund**: a pooled vehicle run by an AMC under a trust, regulated by SEBI. Investors hold units. Value per unit is the NAV.
- **NAV**: value of one unit, from the scheme's holdings minus expenses, usually declared each business day.
- **AUM**: total assets a scheme or fund house manages. It moves with both investor flows and market prices. A fall in AUM is not the same as money leaving.
- **Mark-to-market (MTM)**: revaluing holdings at current market prices. A fall from MTM is a change in value, not a withdrawal.
- **SIP / STP / SWP**: invest, transfer or withdraw a fixed amount at set intervals. SIP contribution (money going in) is a different figure from SIP assets (value of what has accumulated).
- **Lump sum**: a single investment.
- **Folio**: an investor's account with a fund house. One investor can hold many.
- **Direct vs regular plan**: same scheme; the regular plan includes distributor commission in its expenses. Mention only if a locked fact does.
- **TER / BER**: total expense ratio is the yearly cost as a share of assets. Under the 2026 regulations a Base Expense Ratio is shown separately from statutory charges. Any limits are facts to be sourced, never recalled.
- **Exit load**: a charge for redeeming before a set period, where the scheme has one.
- **Riskometer / risk-o-meter**: SEBI's risk label for a scheme. Never describe a scheme as "safe".
- **Scheme categories**: SEBI groups schemes into broad families. As of the February 2026 categorisation circular these are equity, debt, hybrid, life cycle, and other (fund of funds and passive). Sub-categories and thresholds are facts to be sourced. Do not rank them.
- **Passive**: index funds and ETFs, which track an index rather than pick securities.
- **SIF**: Specialized Investment Fund, a separate higher-ticket structure. **MF Lite**: lighter regime for passive-only fund houses. Describe only what a sourced fact says.
- **KYC and nomination**: investor onboarding requirements; rules change, so source any statement.

## Who is on the other side of the content

- **The MFD** forwards content to clients under their own name. They need content they can defend if a client or SEBI asks "where is this from?"
- **The end client** is usually not an expert. They react to fear (market falls), to jargon, and to comparisons.
- Content should help the MFD **explain**, not **recommend**. The MFD has the client relationship and the suitability duty; the content does not.

## Recurring content moments (use as discovery prompts, then source each one)

- Monthly: AMFI monthly data release (flows, SIP contribution, folios, AUM).
- Regulatory: SEBI regulations, circulars, board decisions, consultation papers; the effect on how MFDs work or what clients see.
- Macro: RBI policy decisions, inflation and growth releases, and what they do and do not mean for fund types.
- Calendar: Union Budget, financial year start and end, tax filing windows, festival seasons, market milestones.
- Behaviour: market fall or rally weeks, SIP stoppage questions, "should I wait" questions, new investor onboarding.
- Product-literacy: how to read a factsheet, what a riskometer means, what TER is.

## Framings to prefer

- "What the data shows" over "what you should do".
- "What changed / what stayed the same" for regulation.
- "A question your client may ask, and how to explain it".
- Both sides of a movement (for example contributions and asset value) so no single number misleads.

## Framings to avoid

- Predicting markets, returns, flows or regulatory outcomes.
- Telling anyone to start, stop, increase or switch an investment.
- Ranking or comparing named schemes or fund houses, or implying one is better.
- Using a recent fall or rise as proof that an approach "works" or "fails".
- Explaining **why** something happened when no source says why. Report the data and stop.
- Tax advice, product suitability, and guaranteed or implied outcomes.
