# Content writer craft

This skill governs **how** a script reads. The engine and the approved facts govern **what** it may say. When the two seem to conflict, the facts and the checks win and the line is rewritten.

## The reader and the job

The reader is a client of an MFD, or the MFD themself. The content should leave them understanding one thing they can repeat in a sentence. It is not trying to sell, urge or impress.

## House voice

- Plain, calm, factual. Short sentences. One idea per beat.
- Prefer concrete words over abstract ones. "SIP assets fell" over "headwinds affected the asset base".
- Active voice. Say who did what: "AMFI reports", "SEBI notified".
- Keep the source's hedges. "Mainly due to" stays "mainly due to". "Can" stays "can".
- No hype, urgency, fear, or reassurance language. Do not tell the reader how to feel.
- Indian number style where the fact uses it (lakh, crore), and the rupee sign.
- Explain a technical term the first time it appears, in a few words, unless the audience is clearly expert.

## Plain-word swaps (use the explained form on first use)

| Term | Plain form |
| --- | --- |
| AUM | the total value of money a fund house manages |
| NAV | the value of one unit of a scheme |
| Mark-to-market | holdings revalued at current market prices |
| SIP assets | the current value of what SIPs have built up |
| SIP contribution | money put in through SIPs that month |
| TER | the yearly cost of running a scheme, as a share of its assets |
| Exit load | a fee for redeeming before a set period |
| Folio | an investor's account with a fund house |

## Hook patterns that stay honest

A hook poses the question or tension; it never states a result. Use a required term from the brief. Patterns:

1. **The two numbers**: "Contributions rose. Assets fell. Both are true this month." (only when both are locked facts)
2. **The client question**: "Why did my SIP value drop when I kept investing?"
3. **What changed / what stayed**: "One rule changed. Here is what it does not touch."
4. **The common mix-up**: "Two different numbers, one similar name."
5. **The definition**: "What is a Base Expense Ratio?"

Avoid: "You won't believe", "Shocking", "Must know", "Before it's too late", countdowns, or any claim in the hook that no fact backs.

## Beat craft

- **Point**: claim first, qualifier second. One fact per beat.
- **Proof**: show it the same way the source does. Do not add examples with invented figures.
- **Recap**: the core message in nearly the same words as the hook's answer. Repetition here is intended.
- **CTA**: an invitation to save, share or ask the distributor. Never to invest.
- Cut every beat that does not serve a locked key point.

## Format notes

- **Carousel**: slide 1 is the disclosure slot, slide 2 the hook. One idea per slide. About 20 words per slide is a good target; the limit is 30. End on the recap.
- **Reels / video**: follow `skills/shared-reel-craft/SKILL.md`. Written to be spoken. Short sentences, no parentheses, no tables, numbers said in the way people say them. About 60 seconds is roughly 150 words.
- **Infographic**: titled sections, each with one figure and one sentence of meaning.
- **Minimalist**: one statement and its source line. Nothing else.
- **Presentation / ebook / podcast**: only when the brief says so; keep one message per section.

## Regulation explainers (what changed, what stayed)

1. Name the instrument and the date from the source.
2. State what the change does, in one sentence, in the source's terms.
3. State what it does not change, only if a fact says so. If none does, say nothing.
4. State when it takes effect, only as precisely as the source does. A formula such as "on the 180th day from publication" is not a date; do not convert it unless the human supplies the confirmed date.
5. Do not tell the MFD what to do about it. Point them to the official document.

## Data explainers (what the numbers show)

1. Date every number with the period it covers.
2. Give the change and the base together ("up 0.6% from August").
3. If two numbers move in different directions, say so and keep them separate.
4. Do not give a reason for a movement unless the source does.
5. Say what the data does not show if a fact allows it.

## Before handing a script over

Read it aloud. Then check:

- Could a reader repeat the core message in one sentence?
- Does any line sound like advice, a forecast, or a ranking?
- Is any number or date missing from a cited fact?
- Is any term used without being explained once?
- Does the disclosure slot come first, unchanged, with no real names or numbers?
- Is the disclaimer present, verbatim, whenever a performance figure appears?

The team's soul-finance-content method is applied through `skills/shared-soul-finance/SKILL.md`: a real reader, one intent, an everyday Indian scene as the hook, one honest proof, the smallest action. Emotion comes from the scene and framing, never from a promise about outcomes.
